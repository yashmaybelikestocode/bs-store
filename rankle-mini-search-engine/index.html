Rank by votes,
not by repetition.

Early search engines trusted the page that repeated your keyword the most, so spammers stuffed keywords. Rankle counts the links pointing to a page instead. Each link is a vote, and a vote from a trusted page counts for more.

Why the old way failed

        
    
          
  • A page that types “pizza” 12 times beats a real pizzeria that says it twice.
    • 
          
  • Anyone can repeat a word 100 times, so it costs spammers nothing.
    • 
          
  • The page judges itself. Nobody else has to vouch for it.
    • 
        

      

      

        
Why PageRank works

        
    
          
  • Other sites decide your rank by linking to you.
    • 
          
  • A vote from an important page carries more weight.
    • 
          
  • Keyword stuffing and links you make to yourself don’t raise your score.

    Under the hood
    PageRank on the link data
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Who links to whom
    
        
        
    Circle size = PageRank · red = likely fake · dashed = links from a fake page
    
      
    
      
    
        
    The formula
    
        
    PR(A) = (1−d)/N + d · Σ PR(B)/Out(B)
    
        
      
          
    1. Every page starts with an equal share: 1/N.
      2. 
          
    2. Each page splits its score evenly among the pages it links to.
      3. 
          
    3. d = 0.85: the surfer follows a link 85% of the time and jumps to a random page 15% of the time.
      4. 
          
    4. Repeat until the scores stop changing.
      5. 
        
    
        
    Random surfer check
    
        
    
        
    Chance it’s real
    
        
    real% = 95·(1−e−1.3·PR·N) + 3·trusted links
    
        
    A trusted link comes from a page that has inbound links itself. With no trusted links, the score is capped at 15%.
